# Move Plate research brief

BiGym 2.0 reports **93/100 on Move Plate** in a pre-release run with an expanded interface. **I found no ≥90% result on released Move Plate using only the three 84×84 RGB views and proprioception.** The first experiment I’d run today is to keep your 71% policy and add visual feedback at the stage where it loses the most episodes. [BiGym 2.0, Table A8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07594v3)

1. **The 93/100 run establishes a useful upper reference.** GPT-6 Astra developed each controller from one demonstration with a 101,000-step budget. The tool-augmented sessions scored **93/100, 18/100, and 12/100**, averaging **41 ± 26%**. The interface offered 640×480 RGB, calibrated rays, world-frame wrist/base positions, differential IK, and rate limiting. The pre-release strict sessions scored **31/100, 40/100, and 47/100**. I found neither the winning program nor an ablation identifying what it relied on. On the released benchmark, π₀.₅ full SFT scored **65%** on Move Plate and **92%** on *Dishwasher Load Cups*, which requires releasing two mugs onto the upper rack. Both used 60 VR demos and 50 episodes at each of five checkpoints from one run. Move Plate ACT scored **60.1%** (**70.0%** mean peak), DP **61.7%**, and CQN-AS **48.2%**, with 100 episodes per checkpoint, five final checkpoints, and three runs. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07594v3) · [Public policy archive](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWIRL-Lab/bigym2-agent-rollouts)

2. **Add feedback gates and hold the base still during contact.** PGX’s simulated G1/Dex-3 with stage-tuned π₀.₅ reached **90% randomized mug placement** versus **0%** flat, using **30 demos**. Bimanual shelf-to-container box transport reached **90% nominal / 60% randomized** versus **10% / 5%** flat, using **100 demos**. The paper reports **at least 20 episodes per cell**. It used object-relative stances, base station keeping, grasp and release checks, and bounded retries. I could not verify whether its simulated pose estimates came entirely from allowed sensors. For your policy, use wrist RGB and finger proprioception to verify a short test lift, then gate slot entry and release on fresh plate-to-rack alignment. [PGX, Table II](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26596v1)

3. **Reacquire the rack after transport and verify support before release.** RPG’s simulated bimanual YAM *Place dish on rack* reached **10/10 held-out**. Changing only the skill library raised **5/10 to 9/10** on paired starts. RPG reviewed **3–8 source trajectories per selected task**, then ran **15 practice rounds** without updating model weights. Its repairs included re-observing after motion and checking reachability through approach, lift, placement, and retreat. It used RGB-D and allowed 20 minutes per episode. Separately, ABC-Sim QF3’s three-plate task used **50 episodes per checkpoint** and reduced median seated tilt **14° → 9°** and fallout **21% → 8%**. I found no ≥90% end-to-end dish-rack score for QF3. [RPG, Tables II and IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02204v1) · [QF3, §5.2](https://qf3-rl.github.io/data/qf3.pdf)

4. **Use the placement comparators to choose what to borrow.** In Isaac Lab-Arena, GR00T 2 put a box from table to shelf on a Unitree G1 in **90/100** episodes versus **77/100** for GR00T 1.7, with **100 demos per task**. It jointly predicts video and actions above a lower-body controller. On RoboCasa GR-1, ACE-Ego-0 transferred an object between two plates in **49/50** trials, using **1,000 demos per task** across 24 tasks plus large-scale pretraining. It uses camera-frame end-effector commands and reliability-weighted human pseudo-actions. [GR00T 2, Table 34](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-2/assets/gr00t2_whitepaper.pdf) · [ACE-Ego-0, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17200v1)

5. **Lower-resolution RGB and stage-specific training have precedents.** RoboMimic’s fixed-base bimanual *Transport* reached **100% peak / 93% late** with visual CNN Diffusion Policy, **200 human demos**, and **50 rollouts × three training seeds per checkpoint**. It used four roughly 84px views, predicted 16 actions, and replanned after eight. BEHAVIOR-1K’s Cosmos WAM scored **8/10, 10/10, 8/10** on *Putting dishes away after cleaning* across three public validation rounds, with **200 demos/task**, stage prediction, and skill-specific LoRA. I found no private per-task score. LIBERO-PRO’s RGB GLaD put a wine bottle on a rack at **92%** with **50 demos and 50 trials**, falling to **0%** under position perturbation. I found no verified ≥90% RGB/proprio result for ManiSkill’s standard 3 mm peg insertion or a comparable binary placement result in HumanoidBench. [Diffusion Policy](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/diffusion_policy_2023.pdf) · [BEHAVIOR](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13818v1) · [GLaD](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09619v1) · [ManiSkill insertion](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=e5jGTEiJMT) · [HumanoidBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10506v2)

**Today’s test.** Log the first failed stage on development seeds. If insertion dominates, choose the most reachable of the six valid slots, servo the plate’s lower rim and plane against rack features, and reserve 50 steps after release. Use privileged state to label development rollouts and train the visual estimates. Freeze the controller before scoring 100 held-out episodes. Moving from 71% to 90% requires recovering roughly **two-thirds of current failures** if there are zero regressions. [Task checker](https://bigym2.github.io/docs/tasks.html#move-plate)
